# Guide refinement playbook — September 20, 2026

This records the user's decisions from the KHFM refinement session and supersedes older conflicting presentation and optional-inventory proposals. Apply to every game with game-specific mechanics. Data Jiminy is explicitly excluded from this implementation cycle; retain existing KHFM behavior without extending it.

## Answers first

Use one descriptive title, with relevant counts alongside it. Remove eyebrow/title/subtitle marketing stacks, motivational copy, decorative explanatory captions, and redundant repeated counts. Descriptions must answer an actual player question. Preserve the journal's serif headings, readable body typography, paper surfaces, semantic accent colors and compact spacing. Increase readability by reducing padding rather than shrinking text. Minimum 16px inputs and 44px interactive targets; phone layouts must fit without horizontal scrolling.

## Navigate by the thing being collected

Each meaningful game-specific collection gets a primary destination. Group entries by world/area in progression order and support search, world filters and remaining/completed filters. Worlds is a hub with general information and links to filtered collection pages. Do not force a player collecting one category across worlds through a chain of world detail pages. Use distinctive menu icons. Preserve each game's mechanics and separate character/campaign scopes where appropriate.

Use single-column rows with inline expandable detail. Keep multiple entries open, provide expand/collapse all, and make completion checks independent of expansion. Expanded details should include location, actionable acquisition instructions, real prerequisites, rewards and meaningful missability; omit empty or repetitive boilerplate. Keep useful related links. Retain stable entry identities across views and progress.

Backup approaches, only if inline expansion proves unsatisfactory: (1) compact item detail modals; (2) a detailed category table for a selected world. Neither is the default.

## Workshops and planning

Where the game actually crafts, use Recipes → Materials → Farming Plan. Tracking is always available; no opt-in switch. Sort materials by elemental/type families and rank. Collapsed rows show source, drop percentage or “Conditional,” farm location, owned stock and Add to farming plan. Put conditional rules and finite treasure alternatives in expanded details. Do not repeat generic drop-boost advice on every item.

Adding a recipe adds the direct ingredients for one craft to material targets; repeated additions accumulate. Adding a material directly establishes a target without resetting an existing target. Show target, owned and remaining in the farming plan, with all source/location/expanded information inline. Unknown stock differs from zero. Historical crafting checks do not consume inventory. Do not invent synthesis for games without it; adapt command melding or spirit creation to their actual rules, preserving modifiers and character scopes.

## Trustworthy content and persistence

Start from the checked-in per-game research, specifications and legacy inventories. Preserve provenance and uncertainty. Never invent precise drops, locations or counts to fill a gap; source and document conflicts. Counts describe actual represented records and never imply an incomplete catalog is complete. Distinguish acquisition events from reward units and avoid double counting.

Player state is game-scoped, with character scope when required, persistent completion/stock/targets, backups and recovery. Keep canonical IDs stable. Import validation must reject another game's profile, preserve unknown stock and bound quantities. Failed saves must be visible. Reopening and switching games must not mix progress.

## Delivery and review

Research the game's available facts and mechanics, record a plan, implement functional routes and content, then verify build, calculations, persistence and desktop/phone layouts. No new assistant/model/retrieval work. Do not count a placeholder or a list of feature promises as a finished guide. Document actual content coverage and unresolved factual gaps clearly in maintenance notes. Save feedback and implementation lessons in the repository, commit the work and report validation honestly.
